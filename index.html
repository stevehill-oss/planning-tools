Insights and guidance

What to think about before you choose

Short, practical notes from the questions people ask our specialists most.

Concerts

Choosing where to sit at a concert

Decide first whether you came for the sound, the sight of the stage or the feeling of the crowd. The answer changes the seat.

The floor puts you closest, but it is usually standing and the view depends on who is in front of you. Raised seats in the lower tier look over the crowd and keep the stage in full view. A private suite sits further back and side-on, and trades distance for space, comfort and somewhere to talk.

If anyone in your group needs to sit for the whole show, rule out standing areas early.

Value

Is hospitality worth it?

It depends on what you would spend on the day anyway, and what you value beyond the seat.

Add up a standard ticket plus the food, drink, travel and time you would spend around it. Then compare that with a package that includes some of those things. The gap is what you pay for comfort, a better seat and a smoother day.

The Is Hospitality Worth It? tool does this with your own figures, and lets you put a value on the things that matter to you.

Comparison

Standard ticket or hospitality?

The event is the same. What changes is everything around it.

Hospitality packages typically change how you arrive, where you sit, where you eat and who looks after you. A standard ticket gives you entry and a seat, and the rest of the day is yours to arrange.

Inclusions differ by venue and package, so check exactly what is included before comparing prices.

Corporate

Seating for client entertaining

If the day is about the relationship, choose the space before the seat.

A private box or suite gives you somewhere quiet to talk and a host who handles the details. A shared lounge suits larger groups and a livelier feel, with less privacy. Think about how long your guests will spend at the table compared with in their seats.

Some organisations have a policy on accepting hospitality. It is worth checking with your guests before you invite them.

Planning

Where the time goes on the day

Much of a big event is spent getting in, finding food and getting out. Planning that part changes the day.

With dining booked, a reserved table and a host who knows the venue, more of your time goes on the event and your guests. Where a package includes fast-track or dedicated entry, arriving is simpler too.

How much time it saves depends on the venue and the day, so ask a specialist what applies to your event.

Questions

Straight answers

Anything else, call a specialist on 0333 305 5377.

Are seat views guaranteed?

No. The Seat Reality Viewer is an illustrative 3D model that shows the kind of view each area gives, not an exact rendering of a venue. A hospitality specialist confirms the exact seats and what you will see before you book.

Do I need an account to use the tools?

No. Every tool works straight away with no login. Your brief is kept in this browser only.

Are the scores facts?

No. View, sound, comfort and atmosphere scores are indicative opinions for comparing one area with another. They are not measurements and can differ from venue to venue.

Do the calculators use your prices?

No. The calculators use the figures you type in. Any example figures are illustrative and clearly labelled, not quotes. A specialist gives you real prices for your date and group.

Can I share my results?

Yes. Every result has its own link that opens the tool with your answers filled in, and you can collect results in My brief to copy, print or send to a specialist.
